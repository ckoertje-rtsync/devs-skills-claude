# SES/MB Tools, Representations, and Extensions

This file covers the software that implements the System Entity Structure / Model Base (SES/MB) framework, from Zeigler-lineage tools to third-party extensions. For each tool it says what it adds to or changes in the classic formalism and how it represents an SES. The 1980s–1990s lineage (ESP-4, ESP-Scheme, DEVS-Scheme, MODSYN, FRASES) is in `history.md`. MS4 Me behaviors are detailed in `ms4me-facts.md`. Current as of 2026-10-06.

Provenance tags: [Z] Zeigler or coauthors, read directly; [T] third-party literature; [G] MS4 Me User's Guide (page); [F] field observation from this repo's practice; [I] inference no source states.

## 2. MS4 Me in brief

MS4 Me is the current Zeigler-lineage tool [Z/G]. It writes the SES and the PES as controlled English, and every statement ends with `!`:

- **Aspects:** `from the X perspective, Y is made of A, B and C!`
- **Multi-aspects:** `... is made of more than one U!`
- **Specializations:** `E can be A or B in LABEL!`
- **Couplings:** `A sends M to B [as N]!`, with `all`/`each` over copies.

Pruning lives in a separate `.pes` file, and `write class files!` generates Java DEVS classes [G pp. 16–17, 128, 173–178]. Zeigler & Sarjoughian's *Guide to Modeling and Simulation of Systems of Systems* teaches the SES in MS4 Me terms. Its chapters include "System Entity Structure Basics," "Specialization and Pruning," "Aspects and Multi-aspects," "Managing Inheritance in Pruning," and "Automated and Rule-Based Pruning [and Experimental Execution]" ([2013 ed.](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-85729-865-2); [2017 ed.](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-64134-8)) [Z]. MS4 Systems also ran a cloud "Model Store" for sharing model suites built by SES merging (Zeigler, Seo & Kim 2013, IJMSSC 4(3):1340006, [doi:10.1142/S1793962313400060](https://doi.org/10.1142/S1793962313400060)) [Z].

Three MS4 Me conventions depart from the textbook view of pruning as selection only [G/I]:

- Multi-aspects expand through an index specialization, and restructuring moves the copies up to the grandparent's aspect.
- Unmade choices are filled at random.
- A selected specialization has empty behavior unless the PES says `inherit from`.

For the full set of verified behaviors with page numbers, read `ms4me-facts.md`. For exact syntax, regexes and validators, use the `ses-designer` skill: `resources/reference/ses-grammar.md`, `pes-grammar.md`, `ses-axioms.md`, and `scripts/validate-ses.sh` / `validate-pes.sh`.

## 3. The two XML traditions

Two different XML mappings exist. **Do not conflate them** [I].

**(a) The SES generates a domain XML Schema** (Zeigler & Hammonds; reused by Mittal and Risco-Martín). Zeigler & Hammonds 2007, *Modeling & Simulation-Based Data Engineering* (Academic Press, ISBN 9780123725158), has chapters "Representing the SES in XML" and "Using the SES-Java Tool Set," and the publisher blurb targets automating "the development of XML dtds and schemas" [Z, publisher page and table of contents]. Risco-Martín, de la Cruz, Mittal & Zeigler (2009, eUDEVS, *SIMULATION* 85(11–12):750–777, [doi:10.1177/0037549709104727](https://doi.org/10.1177/0037549709104727)) say [Z]: "Tools have been developed to transform SESs back and forth to XML ... it makes development of XML Schema transparent to the modeler ... SES structures are compact relative to equivalent Schema." In this tradition:

- Behavior travels separately, in XFD-DEVS, an XML Schema for finite deterministic DEVS.
- A model synthesized from a PES "doesn't contain the behavior specifications."
- What is preserved: composition and taxonomy, as data structure.
- What is lost: dynamics.

The group's own statement of the relationship is a mapping (SES to schema or DTD, PES to an instance document; Cheon, Kim & Zeigler 2008). It is not an expressiveness ranking. Do not claim the SES is "more expressive than XML Schema" [Z].

**(b) The SES itself is an XML instance of a fixed meta-schema** (DLR / TU Clausthal SESTools; Wismar also exchanges XML). `ses.xsd` (XML Schema 1.1) defines `entity`, `aspect`, `multiAspect`, `specialization`, and `var`, where `var` carries `name/type/default/lower/upper` [T]. Three `xs:assert` rules check:

1. that no descendant repeats an entity's name (strict hierarchy),
2. that sibling names are distinct (valid brothers),
3. that variable names are distinct (attached variables).

Selection constraints are XPath [T].

- **What this mapping preserves:** tree shape, alternating mode (structurally), three axioms, and variable bounds [I].
- **What it loses:**
  - couplings, since there is no element for them;
  - the uniformity check;
  - port and message information;
  - procedural knowledge.
- **A side effect:** `minOccurs="0"` allows empty aspects and specializations [I].

A 2019 proposal for a "standard computational representation" (Karmokar, Durak, Hartmann & Zeigler, SummerSim 2019, per the [DLR elib record](https://elib.dlr.de/130764); no DOI found, venue UNVERIFIED) motivated this line, and it says the lack of a standard "hinders the shareability of SES artifacts and interoperability of SES tools" [Z]. Its full text was not read.

## 4. The Wismar extended SES (eSES/MB)

Hochschule Wismar, working with DLR and with Zeigler, built the most substantial third-party extension [T/Z]. The constructs below come from Pawletta, Schmidt, Durak & Zeigler 2018 (SNE 28(1):11–18, [doi:10.11128/sne.28.tn.10402](https://doi.org/10.11128/sne.28.tn.10402), pp. 13–17) and Folkerts, Pawletta, Deatcu & Durak 2017 (SNE 27(4):167–176, [doi:10.11128/sne.27.tn.10391](https://doi.org/10.11128/sne.27.tn.10391)). SESFunctions were first proposed in Pawletta, Schmidt, Zeigler & Durak 2016 (SpringSim-ANSS, pp. 62–69, [doi:10.22360/springsim.2016.anss.061](https://doi.org/10.22360/springsim.2016.anss.061)).

| Construct | What it does |
|---|---|
| **SES variables** | Global, uppercase "input interface of an SES" (e.g. `SPEC_CTRL ∈ {'lc','nc'}`) |
| **Semantic conditions** | Checked before pruning "to exclude certain system configurations" |
| **Selection rules** | On specializations: `srule = { SPEC_CTRL=='lc' → dtfcn \| ... }`; required for automated pruning (SNE 33(1)) |
| **Aspect rules** | Choose among sibling aspect or multi-aspect nodes on one level |
| **Selection constraints** | Select an entity "based on decisions taken anywhere else in the SES tree" |
| **Priorities** | Order among descriptive nodes on one level |
| **`numRep`** | Number of replications at a multi-aspect |
| **`mb` attribute** | Leaf-only link into the model base (e.g. `{mb='scope'}`) |
| **Couplings** | 4-tuples `{'SrcEntity','FromPrt','SinkEntity','ToPrt'}` covering IC, EIC, EOC; "causal or acausal" |
| **SESFunctions** | Procedural code (MATLAB or Python) evaluated during pruning. They "extend the declarative specification defined by the baseline SES by procedural knowledge descriptions" (SNE 28(1), p. 14) |
| **NONE element** | Optional features: "if the NONE branch is selected, the entity is not included at all" (SNE 27(4), p. 170) |
| **Merge** | Combines SES trees at a merge point with the same entity name |
| **Flattening** | Reduces a PES to an FPES for model building |
| **Experiment Control / Execution Unit** | The EC "computes new settings for SESVariables" for the next cycle, and the EU runs (model, experimental frame) tuples. Together they support reactive, automated variant generation (SNE 28(1), p. 17; Pawletta, Durak & Schmidt 2019, [doi:10.1016/B978-0-12-813543-3.00018-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-813543-3.00018-4)) |

**Relation to the axioms.** The group treats Zeigler's axioms as validity criteria [T]:

- It claims "a complete solution without violation of the strict hierarchy axiom" for the variable-decomposition problem that Rozenblit & Zeigler (1993) had handled by relaxing the axiom.
- It renames replicas (`s1, s2, s3`) "to comply with the valid brothers axiom."
- It combines parent and selected child "according to the inheritance axiom" into names like `sine_s1` (SNE 28(1), pp. 14–16).

**Feature-model mapping** [T] (Folkerts et al. 2017):

| Feature-model relation | SES form |
|---|---|
| Mandatory (AND) | Aspect |
| Alternative (XOR) | Specialization |
| Optional | NONE element |
| OR | An aspect with two or more specializations, each with a NONE child |

So classic SES covers AND and XOR natively but not optional or OR [I]. Pawletta et al. 2018 frame SES/MB against software product lines: a metamodel processed by transformation versus a "150% model" with variation points [T].

**Head-to-head evidence.** Deatcu, Pawletta & Folkerts (2019, SNE 29(1):39–43, [doi:10.11128/sne.29.sw.10466](https://doi.org/10.11128/sne.29.sw.10466)) compared Simulink Variant Manager with SESToPy [T]:

- Variant Manager interfaces are static, "while the eSES/MB approach allows to define variable interfaces and couplings."
- Variant Manager modeling is bottom-up, and eSES/MB is top-down.
- The authors concede "a final comparison is not possible on the basis of just one example."

**Status** [T/I]. The Wismar SES repositories show no pushes after mid-2022, the latest SES paper found is 2023 (Folkerts, Pawletta & Durak, SNE 33(1):17–25, [doi:10.11128/sne.33.tn.10633](https://doi.org/10.11128/sne.33.tn.10633)), and the same GitHub organization kept updating DEVS-for-MATLAB repositories into 2025–2026. eSES moves the SES from a declarative family specification to a parameterized, partly procedural metamodel. That makes pruning automatable, but the SES is then only as analyzable as the embedded code [I].

## 5. Where the tools disagree

All tools agree on the four node types and on pruning in outline [I].

| Question | MS4 Me | eSES (Wismar) | SESTools (DLR / TU Clausthal) | Zeigler–Hammonds XML |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ports vs messages | Message names become `out<Msg>`/`in<Msg>` ports [G pp. 122–123] | Explicit port names in 4-tuples | No coupling element | Data schema; behavior separate (XFD-DEVS) |
| Where couplings live | Statements scoped to an aspect label [G p. 176] | `cplg` attribute on aspect and multi-aspect nodes, static or computed | Absent | n/d (chapter not read) |
| Where multiplicity lives | PES (`set multiplicity`) via an index specialization; tree restructured [G p. 121] | `numRep` node attribute, often from SES variables | Set during pruning | n/d |
| Pruning control | Interactive or textual; random completion [G pp. 122, 168] | Rule- and function-driven; semantic conditions first | Interactive, XPath-constrained | n/d |
| Constraint language | Ranges; `don't select` [G pp. 129, 177] | Rules, constraints, priorities in MATLAB/Python expressions | XPath | n/d |
| Code inside the SES | No | Yes (SESFunctions) | No | No |
| File format | English `.ses`/`.pes` | Toolbox internals; positional JSON plus Python | XML instance of `ses.xsd` | SES ↔ XML Schema |
| Inheritance naming | Prefixed copy names (`tall_Boy`); behavior needs `inherit from` [G pp. 26–27, 124] | Parent and child combined (`sine_s1`) | n/d | n/d |

Two consequences follow [I]:

- **Coupling semantics are the least standardized part.** MS4 Me's message ports, eSES's computed tuples, and SESTools' absent couplings cannot be translated into one another without loss. Any exchange standard has to settle ports versus messages and static versus computed couplings first.
- **Determinism differs.** MS4 Me tolerates an incomplete PES and fills it at random, while eSES and SESTools aim for fully determined pruning. For reproducible experiments, an MS4 Me PES should resolve every choice explicitly.

## 6. Gaps

- **OWL.** No verified paper maps SES constructs to OWL classes and properties. One healthcare application links an SES-based ontology to OWL (Djitog & Kah 2020, see `applications.md`). arXiv:1602.00242 is a false friend: there "SES" means socio-ecological system [T].
- **SysML.** No verified SysML import or export tool exists. SES-X uses the X language, not SysML [T].
- **Python packages.** No package named "pyses" and no SES package on PyPI were found. "Python SES" in the literature means the Wismar SESToPy/SESMoPy suite [T].
- **VLE.** No evidence of SES support was found. Current DEVS-Suite releases were not checked for an SES editor. The SESM paper (I/ITSEC 2005) was not opened (UNVERIFIED).
- **Mapping rules.** The exact SES → XML Schema mapping rules in Zeigler & Hammonds ch. 3 (specializations to `xs:choice`? multi-aspects to `maxOccurs`? couplings?) are **unconfirmed**; the chapter was not accessible. The 2019 standard-representation paper was also unread.
- **Records with conflicting details:**
  - SpringSim 2020 "Automated ... Reactive Pruning": Crossref title only, authors UNVERIFIED.
  - Folkerts et al. 2019: same title at IIPhDW 2019 (Crossref) and ASIM 2019 (README).
  - Mod4Sim 2018 "Design Patterns for Variability Modeling": authors UNVERIFIED.
  - The AUTSYM 2017 Wismar workshop venue: UNVERIFIED.
- **Unsupported constructs.** MS4 Me lists "Similarities" ("a truck is like a car") as an SES element but documents no syntax for it [G p. 173]. The semantics of `mergeAll from X.ses!` are not explained [G pp. 27, 129].
- **No cross-tool comparison.** No paper compares MS4 Me, the SES Toolbox, SESToPy, and SESTools. §5 is synthesized from each tool's own documentation.
